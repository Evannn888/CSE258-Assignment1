CSE258 Assignment 1 - write-up (v5)

General
All models use only the designated training CSV of each task. Test files are read for their queries
(user/item ids, timestamps, candidate pairs), never for labels. assignment1.py regenerates every
prediction file; validation numbers below come from held-out training data, not the leaderboard.

Two facts about the data drive several choices:
(1) In Tasks 2, 4 and 5 the negative candidate is drawn in proportion to item popularity: the
    popularity distribution of the test candidates is identical to that of training interactions.
    Raw popularity is therefore useless; every feature is a lift (evidence divided by popularity),
    and validation negatives are sampled the same way.
(2) The Task 1-4 datasets are 10-cores: every item has at least 10 interactions across the training
    file and both test files (checked; no exceptions).

Tasks 1 and 3: rating prediction (unchanged from the previous submission)
The leaderboard ignores ground-truth zero ratings, so the target is the rating given that it is
positive; zero ratings remain implicit interactions. Base learners are shrunk user/item biases,
explicit MF, user-block SVD++, spectral residual profiles, reliable top-K item neighbourhoods,
cumulative-logit rating styles and inferred reading stages (Task 1); Task 3 adds temporal
session/drift random effects. A Ridge model plus a residual gradient-boosted tree (HGB) stacks them
on out-of-sample features. Task 1 averages three cross-fitted base models with a frozen spectral
basis. A simpler leave-one-out GBDT and tuned MF/SVD++ were tried this round but were clearly worse
(MF alone barely beats the bias model on this data; neighbourhood features carry most of the signal).

Tasks 2 and 4: which of two items did the user interact with
Per candidate: item-item co-occurrence evidence aggregated over the user's history (each neighbour
who shares a history item and has the candidate votes 1/(history length+2), normalised by the
popularity of both items, with rating weights); spectral features from a truncated SVD of the
degree-normalised user-item matrix (user-item lift, similarity to a community centroid, pooled
similarity to the history). Task 2 adds the candidate's activity during the user's active period;
Task 4 weights history items by closeness to the query time, adds the candidate's popularity in
windows around the query time and a user-based neighbourhood weighted by time. Pairs are classified
by an antisymmetric HGB on feature differences plus symmetric context (both orientations are used in
training). Training pairs come from four held-out folds of 30k interactions with two popularity
negatives each. Two exact rules are applied: a candidate already in the user's training history is
the negative (positives are new pairs), and an item whose training + other-test count is below 10
must be a positive in at least that many rows (10-core deficit rule).
Validation accuracy: Task 2 0.941, Task 4 0.936.

Task 5: next item in the sequence
Features: the co-occurrence evidence above with order-aware weights (whether neighbours took the
candidate shortly after the shared item), position statistics of items in histories, the spectral
features (with recency-weighted SVD), and the candidate's popularity in windows after the time of the
user's last training event relative to all activity in those windows (newly popular items are much
more likely to be read next; this was the largest gain, +0.8 points). Training sets mimic the test:
every user's last event is held out, both on the full data and on data truncated at four earlier
cutoffs (2016-07 to 2017-08), with three popularity negatives each. A candidate never seen in
training is always the positive (negatives are training items); a candidate already in the history is
the negative. Validation accuracy 0.927 (train on cutoff sets, test on last events), versus 0.917 for
the previous feature set on the same protocol. Only timestamps of training interactions are used (the
test rows have none); --task5-order-only runs the same model without any timestamp (validation 0.915).

Task 6: time until the final interaction
The test interaction is each user's final event. Pseudo-labels: drop each user's last k-1 training
events (k = 1, 2, 3); the new last event is the target and its gap from the previous event the label.
Features: the user's gap distribution (median, quantiles, recent gaps, burstiness), the time left until
the end of the data, the target item's popularity in windows after the user's last event, quantiles
of the item's interaction times after that event, and how long a break other readers of the item
usually took before reading it. An HGB with absolute-error loss on log(1+gap) predicts the median;
predictions are scaled by 1.05 (the next gap is slightly longer than earlier ones) and capped by the
time left. Validation MAE on last-training-event targets: 7.09M seconds. The previous model trained
only on users still active after artificial cutoffs and predicted gaps about twice as long.
